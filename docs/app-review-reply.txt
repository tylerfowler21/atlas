1. SCREEN RECORDING
Attached. It shows cold launch, Sign in with Apple (registration and login in
one, as a new Apple ID creates the account), saving a place from search,
creating a trip and adding a stop, the Bookings tab, reporting a published
trip, reporting and blocking a person, and account deletion.

2. PURPOSE AND AUDIENCE
Roava is a personal travel map and trip planner. People keep travel notes in
several places at once: a Notes list of restaurants, a spreadsheet for the
itinerary, screenshots in a group chat. None of it is on a map, none of it is
together. Roava puts it in one place: you save places you have been and want
to go, they become pins on your own map, and you build a trip day by day from
them. The audience is ordinary travellers planning their own trips. It is a
single-user tool first; following someone or publishing a trip is optional.
There are no in-app purchases and no paid content.

3. SETUP AND ACCESS
No demo account is needed and none exists, because there is no email-and-
password sign-in. Sign in is Sign in with Apple or Google only. Tap "Continue
with Apple" and you are in: no invite code, waiting list or allowlist. Any
Apple ID gets a full account immediately, with every feature available. Hiding
your email with Apple's private relay works normally.

Main features from a fresh account:
- Map tab: search a place at the top, tap a result, save it. It becomes a pin.
  The button at bottom right centres the map on you, asking for location
  permission the first time.
- Trips tab: "+ New trip". Inside, "Add a stop" searches for places, the
  calendar shows the trip's days in the month they fall in, Bookings collects
  anything ticked as needing one, and "Before you go" is a checklist for apps,
  passes and documents.
- Journal tab: dated entries, optionally attached to a trip.
- Discover tab: "People" lists users you can follow; "Feed" shows trips they
  published, which you can copy or report.
- Account tab: profile, sign out, delete account.
No sample files are needed; the app works with an empty account.

4. EXTERNAL SERVICES
- Vercel: hosts the web API the app talks to (https://www.roava.co)
- Neon: PostgreSQL database holding places, trips and journal entries
- Vercel Blob: storage for photos attached to journal entries
- Sign in with Apple and Google Sign-In: the only authentication. Handled by
  Auth.js; we never see or store a password.
- Apple MapKit: the map, via react-native-maps
- Nominatim (OpenStreetMap Foundation) and Photon (Komoot): worldwide place
  search and geocoding
- Anthropic (Claude API): one optional free feature, "Plan one for me", which
  drafts an itinerary from a destination and a number of days. Only the text
  typed on that screen is sent. Each suggested place is then checked against
  the geocoders above and shown to the user to confirm before it is saved.
- Expo Application Services: delivers JavaScript updates to the build
No advertising, analytics or tracking SDKs. The app collects no data for
tracking as defined by App Tracking Transparency and shows no ATT prompt.

5. REGIONAL DIFFERENCES
None. The app behaves identically everywhere: same features, same content, no
geographic restrictions or region-locked functionality. Maps and place search
cover the whole world. Dates and place names follow the device's locale, the
only thing that varies; no feature is added or removed anywhere.

6. REGULATED INDUSTRY / THIRD-PARTY MATERIAL
Neither applies. Roava is not a booking agent, does not sell travel, processes
no payments and holds no licence-requiring role. The Bookings feature is a
personal checklist of things booked elsewhere, with a field for the user's own
confirmation number; nothing is booked through the app. It hosts no protected
third-party material: content is the user's own, or map data used under its
providers' licences — Apple MapKit under Apple's terms, OpenStreetMap-derived
geocoding under the Open Database Licence, attributed in the app.
